🧪 Organic Chemistry – Basic Principles

Organic chemistry is the branch of chemistry that deals mainly with carbon-containing compounds.

Classification a IUPAC Isomerism Electronic Effects Mechanisms Reactions Formula Sheet MCQs

1. 📖 Introduction to Organic Chemistry

Organic chemistry deals with the structure, properties, composition, reactions and preparation of carbon compounds.

Carbon is special because of its ability to form strong covalent bonds with itself and with many other elements. This property is called catenation.

⭐ Why is Carbon Special?

  • Carbon has valency 4.
  • It forms stable C–C bonds.
  • It can form single, double and triple bonds.
  • It forms chains, branches and rings.
  • It forms compounds with H, O, N, S and halogens.

4. 📝 IUPAC Nomenclature

IUPAC nomenclature provides a systematic method for naming organic compounds.

Basic Steps

  1. Select the longest continuous carbon chain.
  2. Identify the principal functional group.
  3. Number the carbon chain.
  4. Identify substituents.
  5. Assign appropriate locants.
  6. Write the final name.

Example

CH₃–CH₂–CH₂–CH₃

Four carbon atoms → prefix but-

All single bonds → -ane

Name = Butane

Branched Example

   CH₃
   |
CH₃–CH–CH₃

Longest chain = 3 carbon atoms.

Methyl group attached to carbon 2.

Name = 2-Methylpropane

6. ⚡ Electronic Effects

Electronic effects explain how electrons are distributed within organic molecules and strongly influence their reactivity.

6.1 Inductive Effect

The permanent displacement of sigma electrons along a carbon chain due to an electronegative atom or group is called the inductive effect.

-I effect: –NO₂, –CN, –COOH, –F, –Cl

+I effect: Alkyl groups

6.2 Resonance

Resonance occurs when a molecule can be represented by two or more contributing structures with the same arrangement of atoms but different electron distributions.

⭐ Resonance generally stabilizes a molecule or ion.

6.3 Hyperconjugation

Hyperconjugation is the delocalization of sigma electrons, usually involving C–H bonds adjacent to a π system or electron-deficient carbon.

6.4 Electromeric Effect

The complete transfer of a π-electron pair toward one atom under the influence of an attacking reagent is called the electromeric effect.

7. ⚙️ Organic Reaction Mechanism

Bond Fission

Homolytic Fission

In homolytic cleavage, each atom takes one electron from the bond.

A–B → A• + B•

This produces free radicals.

Heterolytic Fission

In heterolytic cleavage, both bonding electrons go to one atom.

A–B → A⁺ + B⁻

This produces ions.

Electrophiles

Electrophiles are electron-deficient species that accept an electron pair.

H⁺, NO₂⁺, BF₃

Nucleophiles

Nucleophiles are electron-rich species that donate an electron pair.

OH⁻, CN⁻, NH₃

8. ⚗️ Important Types of Organic Reactions

Addition Reaction

CH₂=CH₂ + H₂ → CH₃–CH₃

Substitution Reaction

CH₄ + Cl₂ → CH₃Cl + HCl

Elimination Reaction

An elimination reaction generally removes atoms/groups from adjacent carbon atoms and can form a multiple bond.

Oxidation

Oxidation in organic chemistry often involves an increase in oxygen content or decrease in hydrogen content.

Reduction

Reduction often involves an increase in hydrogen content or decrease in oxygen content.

⚠️ Concept Traps

  • Do not confuse electrophiles with nucleophiles.
  • Electrophiles accept electron pairs.
  • Nucleophiles donate electron pairs.
  • Homolytic cleavage forms radicals.
  • Heterolytic cleavage forms ions.
  • Always select the longest appropriate carbon chain when applying IUPAC rules.
  • Do not confuse molecular formula with structural formula.
